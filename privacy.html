NY Lottery Calculator & Portfolio — Privacy Policy

Effective August 22, 2026

Both pages — the after-tax calculator and the lump-sum portfolio model — run entirely in your browser. There is no account, no sign-in and no server of ours holding anything.

What These Pages Store

Two kinds of thing, both in your browser's local storage and both deleted by clearing the site's browser data. First, your own settings: the theme you picked, the jackpot game and number of winners you last used, which payout you were asking about, your filing status and whether you live in New York City or Yonkers, which sections you left open, and the portfolio page's slider values (take-home amount, spending, inflation, yields and the equity split). The filing status and the town are kept only so the two pages agree with each other and so you don't have to answer twice; they are two picks from two short lists and they never leave your device. The figures you type are never sent anywhere — every calculation happens on your device. Second, a copy of the public figures the pages fetch — the current jackpots, the latest winning numbers and the Treasury yield — kept for up to six hours so a repeat visit doesn't re-query the feeds. Each figure is shown with its date, and if a refresh fails the page keeps showing the earlier figures and says so, rather than going blank.

Both pages also keep a copy of themselves on your device, so they open offline: the pages, the stylesheet, the tax tables and the icon — the same public files anyone can read on GitHub, and nothing else. None of your figures go there, and neither do the jackpots, winning numbers or yields; those live only in the six-hour store above, so once it lapses an offline page tells you it couldn't load them rather than inventing a current-looking number. Clearing the site's browser data removes the copy along with everything else.

Links You Share

Both pages can turn what is on screen into a link. The figures travel inside the link itself — nothing is uploaded, no copy is kept anywhere, and there is no server that could be asked for it later. That also means there is no way to withdraw one once you have sent it, so treat a link the way you would treat emailing a spreadsheet.

Opening someone else's link is deliberately a look rather than a save: the page shows their figures, says at the top that it did, and stores none of them, so whatever you had saved is exactly as you left it when you go back to it.

What Leaves Your Browser

Current jackpots. The calculator asks a small Cloudflare Worker (lottery-proxy.charlie-adams-176.workers.dev) for the current Powerball and Mega Millions jackpots. It reads them from a public lottery site and passes the two numbers back. Nothing about you or your figures is included in the request.

Winning numbers. Recent draw results for six New York games — Powerball, Mega Millions, NY Lotto, Take 5, Pick 10 and Millionaire for Life — come from New York State's own open-data service at data.ny.gov. Only the game you are looking at is ever asked for, and only when you look at it. Again, just a request for public results — nothing of yours goes with it.

T-bill yield. The portfolio page reads the current Treasury yield curve from home.treasury.gov so the cash bucket starts at a real rate.

Those three are the only network calls either page makes, and the pages' Content-Security-Policy blocks anything else outright. Like any web request, each one necessarily shows the service your IP address — that is all they receive.

What These Pages Do Not Do

  • No analytics or usage-tracking scripts
  • No advertising or ad networks
  • No cookies, no accounts, no sign-in
  • No third-party code — the chart library is a copy served from this site, not a CDN
  • No selling or sharing data with third parties; there is nothing to sell

Changes to This Policy

If this policy changes, the updated version will be posted here with a new effective date.
